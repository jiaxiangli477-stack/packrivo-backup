## Ring box styles and inserts: what to pick first

A ring box is judged in the hand, in about three seconds. Whether that moment is a proposal, a boutique counter or a courier envelope decides the style before any artwork is drawn. The four formats below cover most jewelry programs, and each one carries a different insert logic.

- **Lid-and-base**: a two-piece rigid box, board walls wrapped in paper, velvet or PU. Lowest tooling of the rigid family, classic retail feel, and the lid comes off in the buyer's hand rather than swinging open.
- **Magnetic**: the same footprint as lid-and-base with magnets set into the board, so the lid seats itself and resists a knock. Heavier in the hand, better for gift-led SKUs.
- **Drawer**: an inner tray that slides inside a sleeve, often with a ribbon pull or a finger notch. Suits a reveal sequence: sleeve off, tray out, ring already seated.
- **Pillow**: a padded pillow fills the interior and the ring slot sits on top of it. The most forgiving format for a solitaire, a pair of bands or an oversized stone.
- **Clamshell and hinged**: a one-piece construction with a fold hinge. Cheapest to assemble, mailer-friendly, and the usual choice when the box ships inside a mailer rather than being displayed.

Inserts matter as much as the shell. A velvet-covered foam pad with a die-cut slit holds a band without tension; an EVA slit pair holds two rings side by side; a card platform with a clip keeps the stone above the surface so the box can be closed without pressure on the setting; a molded pulp tray is the practical option when the same box also travels as a shipping pack. Pick the insert against the ring style first, then against how the box is opened — the two answers usually eliminate half the formats on their own.

## Materials and print finishes

The wrap decides most of the perceived quality, and the interior decides whether the ring photographs well.

- **Exterior**: velvet (short pile, matte, deep color rendering), PU leather in smooth or grained finishes, linen-textured paper, coated art paper for full-color print. Velvet hides handling marks best; PU wipes clean; printed paper gives the widest artwork range.
- **Interior**: velvet or suede-touch lining over foam, satin for a softer drape, uncoated card when the box is a shipping container rather than a display piece.
- **Finishes**: hot foil stamping in gold, silver, rose gold, copper or matte black; blind emboss and deboss for a tone-on-tone logo; screen print for one or two spot colors on paper wraps; spot UV for a gloss mark on a matte sheet.

Two rules worth checking on a sample. Foil reads best on velvet and on grained PU, and poorly on heavily textured linen; a debossed logo on a dark velvet lid is often more legible than foil at small sizes, because it catches the pile instead of sitting on it. And the interior lining color drives the ring's perceived warmth — a warm off-white lining flatters yellow gold, a cool grey flatters white gold and platinum. Both rules are worth one sample each before committing to a foil plate.

## MOQ, sampling and approval

Printed ring boxes run from **250 pieces per style**, where a style is one size plus one artwork. Unprinted stock boxes start at **100 pieces**. Orders between **100 and 249 pieces** go to manual quoting, because the per-unit economics change and a fixed rate would mislead.

Sampling takes **5 to 10 days**. A sample round normally covers the shell, the wrap and one insert; artwork proofs and foil plates can run in parallel. Sign off on the sample before bulk — the wrap color, the insert tension and the lid fit are the three things that are expensive to correct after production starts.

Every quote ships with a **dieline**, the flat cutting and creasing drawing for the box. The dieline is where artwork placement, foil position and insert clearance are actually defined, so it is the file your designer should be working from rather than a rendered mockup. If a supplier hands you a JPEG and asks for print-ready art, ask for the dieline instead.

## FAQ

**What ring box types can you make?**
Lid-and-base, magnetic, drawer, pillow and clamshell shells, in rigid board or folding board, with velvet, PU, linen or printed paper wraps, and with foam, card, pulp or pillow inserts.

**Can the box carry my logo?**
Yes, as foil stamping, blind emboss or deboss, screen print, or a printed wrap. Foil and emboss both need a plate, which is quoted once; whether it is held for reorders is confirmed on the quote.

**Which insert keeps a ring from moving in transit?**
A die-cut slit pad is the reliable default: the band sits in the slit and cannot rotate. For stones that stand above the band, a card platform with a clip holds the ring off the surface so the lid closes without touching the setting.

**What is a dieline, and do I get one?**
A dieline is the flat cutting and creasing drawing of the box. Every Packrivo quote includes it, so artwork and foil placement are set against the real geometry rather than a mockup.

**How many ring boxes do I have to order?**
250 pieces per style for printed boxes, 100 pieces for unprinted stock. Between 100 and 249 pieces we quote by hand instead of applying the standard rate.

**Is one sample possible before a bulk order?**
Yes. Sampling runs 5-10 days and is quoted on its own, so you can approve wrap, insert and lid fit before production is scheduled. See [samples](/samples/).
