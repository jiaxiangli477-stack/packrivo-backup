## Necklace box formats and anti-tangle inserts

A necklace box has one job that no other jewelry box has: the chain has to stay where it was put. Everything else — the wrap, the foil, the ribbon — is decoration on top of that.

The four formats that work:

- **Long lid-and-base**: the classic presentation box, wide enough for a chain to lie in a curve rather than a coil. Lowest tooling, and the format buyers expect for a pendant presentation gift box.
- **Magnetic long box**: same proportions with a magnet-seated lid. Used when the box is handed over rather than bagged.
- **Drawer**: tray sliding in a sleeve, with the chain laid in the tray and the pendant carded above it.
- **Roll and pillow**: the chain winds around a padded roll or a pillow, which fixes the length and removes the tangle risk entirely, at the cost of a taller box.

Inserts decide the rest. A suede or velvet tray with two notches holds the chain ends at a fixed spread; a card with a top slit and a bottom recess holds the pendant face up and the chain behind it; a foam pad with a curved channel keeps a fixed length of chain in place; a roll holds a whole strand but hides the clasp. For a presentation box where the pendant is the hero, the card-plus-tray combination is the one that photographs cleanly: pendant on top, chain tucked behind the card.

Chain gauge and box depth are the two variables that most often cause fit problems. A fine cable chain in a deep tray sits at the bottom and looks lost; a heavy curb chain in a shallow tray holds the lid off the card. Both problems are visible in one sample — which is why the sample should be loaded with the real product, not a stand-in.

## Materials and print finishes

- **Exterior**: velvet, PU leather, linen paper, printed art paper. Long boxes show wrap seams, so velvet or PU is the safer choice on long formats; printed paper works on shorter and wider shells.
- **Interior**: velvet or suede-touch tray over foam, satin for a soft drape, uncoated card for the pendant card.
- **Finishes**: hot foil stamping, blind emboss and deboss, screen print, spot UV.

Long lids are the reason foil placement needs care. A single centered logo holds on a long lid; a wide foil band tends to lift at the corners as the board relaxes, so foil weight and pressure are set against the length, not against the artwork alone. Deboss on a velvet lid is the alternative that avoids the problem entirely and reads as more restrained.

## FAQ

**How does the insert keep a necklace from tangling?**
By fixing the chain at two points instead of letting it coil. Two notches at a set spread, a curved foam channel, or a roll all do this; the pendant then sits on a card above the chain rather than in it.

**How should a necklace sit in a presentation gift box?**
Pendant face up and visible on opening, chain tucked behind the card, clasp accessible at the side. That sequence is decided by the card and tray, not by the shell, so it is the part worth sampling loaded.

**What size necklace box do I need?**
The longest chain in the range sets the length, the pendant depth sets the interior height. One sample at the longest chain and one at the heaviest pendant answers both.

**Can I add a printed card or a ribbon?**
Yes. A pendant card, care card or EU compliance leaflet can sit behind the tray, and a ribbon or sleeve wraps the shell. Both are quoted as part of the spec, not as afterthoughts.

**What is a dieline and why does the insert need one?**
A dieline is the flat cutting and creasing drawing of the box; on necklace boxes it also defines the insert and card positions, so the pendant sits where the artwork expects it.

**What is the minimum order for custom necklace boxes?**
250 pieces per style for printed boxes, 100 pieces for unprinted stock, and 100-249 pieces quoted by hand rather than at the standard rate. The dieline comes with the quote in every case, so the insert layout is fixed before the run is booked.
