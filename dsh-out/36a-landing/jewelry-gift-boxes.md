## Gift box formats and insert options

A gift box is bought for the moment of opening, so the format decision is really about the opening sequence. Four sequences cover most jewelry programs.

- **Lid-and-base**: lid lifted off, product already visible. The most restrained sequence and the cheapest rigid format; used for fine jewelry where the object should carry the moment rather than the packaging.
- **Magnetic**: lid swings open on a hinge, product revealed at an angle. Reads as the premium gift gesture and is the default for a luxury gift box program.
- **Drawer with ribbon**: sleeve slides off or a tray pulls out by a ribbon. Adds a beat to the opening, which is why it dominates set boxes and seasonal gift editions.
- **Collapsible or foldable**: a rigid-look box that ships flat and assembles on site. The practical answer when storage space is the constraint, at a small cost in stiffness.
- **Pillow**: a padded interior that presents a ring or a pair of earrings face up with the least insert engineering.

Inserts for gift boxes are usually shared across SKUs. A tray with a foam cutout takes a bracelet or a compact necklace; a slit pad takes rings and studs; a card platform holds a pendant or a hooped earring. Designing one insert around the largest item and one around the smallest covers the range without a new tool for every SKU.

## Materials, ribbon and print

- **Exterior**: velvet, PU leather, linen paper, printed art paper. Velvet and PU take handling and travel best; printed paper carries the widest artwork and the lowest cost per unit at volume.
- **Interior**: velvet or suede-touch over foam, satin, uncoated card in collapsible formats.
- **Finishes**: hot foil stamping in gold, silver, rose gold or matte black; blind emboss and deboss; screen print; spot UV. Foil plus deboss on the same lid is a common pairing and needs two passes.
- **Gift dressing**: satin or grosgrain ribbon, printed tissue, a printed insert card, a sleeve. These are quoted as separate components in the same spec so the box and the dressing arrive matched.

Gift dressing is where budgets quietly move. The box is one line; tissue, ribbon, sleeve and card are four more, and they ship as separate items with separate minimums. Ask for them as a single line-item list with quantities against each, and the assembled cost is visible before the order is placed. Ribbon and tissue options are on [custom tissue paper](/printed-tissue-paper/) and [custom bags with logo](/bags-pouches/).

Seasonal gift programs are the case where the calendar, not the artwork, sets the schedule. Production runs **7 to 30 days** after sample sign-off, plus transit, so a gift-season drop that needs a printed sleeve and printed tissue has to start well before the artwork is finished — the tooling and the sample round sit on the critical path, not the printing. Place the sample round against the season, then the bulk order against the sample.

If a gift box runs once a year, the dieline and foil plates are worth holding between runs; a repeat order with unchanged artwork can usually reuse them; what carries over is confirmed on the quote.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between a jewelry gift box and a jewelry box?**
A gift box is specified around the opening sequence and the dressing — lid, tray, ribbon, tissue, card. A storage or display box is specified around keeping the piece safe and visible over time. The shell can be the same; the insert and the dressing differ.

**Why are jewelry boxes so expensive?**
Because most of the cost is not the board. Wrapping, lining, insert tooling, foil plates, ribbon and tissue, plus the low quantities typical of jewelry SKUs, each add a layer. Removing one layer — for instance a plain paper wrap instead of velvet — moves the unit price more than negotiating the board.

**What is foil stamping, and does it work on a gift box lid?**
A heated plate presses metallic foil into the wrap. It works best on velvet, PU and coated paper, and at a single position on a lid; wide foil areas and heavily textured wraps are the two cases where it lifts or fills in.

**Do you supply gift boxes in bulk, or only samples?**
Bulk is the normal case: 250 pieces per style printed, 100 pieces unprinted stock, 100-249 pieces quoted by hand. Samples are quoted separately so the wrap, insert and dressing can be approved first.

**What are rigid boxes, and are they the same as gift boxes?**
Rigid boxes are built on thick board with a wrapped surface, so they hold their shape and do not collapse under a lid. Most gift boxes are rigid; folding cartons are the lighter alternative for high-volume, lower-cost programs.

**How far ahead should a gift-season order start?**
Work backwards from the drop date: transit, then production of 7-30 days, then a sample round of 5-10 days, then artwork and die/plate making. The tooling and sample are the long poles, not the printing.
