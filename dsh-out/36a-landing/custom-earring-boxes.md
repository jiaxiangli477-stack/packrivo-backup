## Earring box formats and inserts

Earring packaging is a quantity problem before it is a design problem. A brand with studs, hoops, drops and huggies across three metal tones is not buying one box, it is buying a size grid and a reorder schedule. The formats below are the ones that survive that reality.

- **Card-backed pack**: a printed card with two punched holes, slid into a clear sleeve or a small folding carton. Cheapest per unit, photographs well flat, and the card doubles as the backer that stops the posts from bending.
- **Small rigid lid-and-base**: board walls, wrapped and lined. The step up when the box is handed over at a counter or shipped as a gift.
- **Magnetic**: same footprint, magnetic lid seating, built the same way as a standard [magnetic jewelry box](/product-magnetic-jewelry-box/). Used when earrings sit in a set alongside a necklace or a bracelet.
- **Drawer**: tray inside a sleeve, used for set boxes and for anything with a card insert behind the earring tray.
- **Pouch or pouch-and-box set**: when the earrings travel with a pouch, the box holds the pouch rather than the jewelry.

Insert logic is different from ring boxes. Earrings need the pair held apart at a fixed spacing, so a die-cut foam pad with two slits, a card with two holes, or a molded pulp tray with two wells all do the job; what changes is how the earrings are seen when the box opens. A pair held on a card reads as retail stock, a pair in a velvet slit pad reads as gift. Studs with butterfly backs need clearance behind the card, drops need depth in the tray, and hoops usually need a wider pad rather than a deeper box.

The same insert geometry is worth checking against [jewelry boxes with fitted inserts](/product-jewelry-box-with-insert/) before the size grid is fixed, and the [jewelry insert fit guide](/guide-jewelry-insert-fit/) lays out how to test one box against several earring styles.

## Materials, cards and print

- **Shell**: velvet, PU leather, linen paper, printed art paper. Velvet is the default for gift positioning; printed paper is the default when the card carries the branding and the box stays plain.
- **Card**: 300-400gsm coated or uncoated card, printed both sides if the card is visible through a sleeve, die-cut with post holes at your earring spacing.
- **Lining**: velvet or suede-touch over foam, uncoated card for card-backed packs.
- **Finishes**: hot foil stamping, blind emboss, screen print, spot UV on paper wraps.

For bulk earring programs the practical trade-off is between card-backed packs and small rigid boxes, and it is a volume decision as much as a look decision. Card packs scale to thousands of units per SKU with one printed sheet; rigid boxes cost more per unit but carry a higher ring at retail and survive courier handling better. Many brands run both: rigid for the hero SKUs and gift sets, card-backed for the long tail of colorways.

## Bulk quantities, sampling and reorders

Printed earring boxes start at **250 pieces per style**; a style is one size plus one artwork. Unprinted stock boxes start at **100 pieces**, which can bridge a launch before the printed run lands. Orders of **100 to 249 pieces** are quoted by hand.

The earring-specific advice is to sample the size grid, not the single box. Send three earring types — a stud, a drop and a hoop — and check that one insert layout holds all three without the pair touching. Sampling runs **5 to 10 days**; what one round covers is set out on [samples](/samples/).

Because earring packaging turns over with colorways and seasonal sets, the reorder path matters more than the first order. Keep the dieline and the foil plate on file; a reorder with unchanged artwork and the same insert can usually reuse the existing plate. Ask on the first quote whether the plate is held for reorders and what a repeat order needs.

## FAQ

**Do you supply earring boxes in bulk for retail brands?**
Yes. Bulk is the normal case here: 250 pieces per style for printed boxes, 100 pieces for unprinted stock, with reorders against the same dieline and foil plate.

**Can one box hold studs, drops and hoops?**
Usually by insert rather than by shell. A two-slit velvet pad covers studs and drops; hoops need a wider pad or a deeper tray. Sample the three earring types together before fixing the size grid.

**Can earring boxes use printed backing cards?**
Yes. Cards print both sides, die-cut with post holes at your spacing, and can carry a barcode, a size grid or care instructions on the reverse.

**What earring box size should I order?**
It follows the largest earring in the family plus insert thickness. The honest answer comes from a sample: send the widest hoop and the longest drop, and the size falls out of the fit check.

**What is foil stamping on a small lid?**
A heated plate presses metallic foil into the wrap. On small earring lids a single-position foil mark works better than a wide design, because the surface area is limited and fine detail fills in.

**How long does a bulk earring box order take?**
Sampling 5-10 days, production 7-30 days after sign-off, then transit. Card-backed packs sit at the short end; rigid boxes with custom inserts at the long end.
