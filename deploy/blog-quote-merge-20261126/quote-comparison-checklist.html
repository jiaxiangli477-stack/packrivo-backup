One supplier quotes a box and insert. Another quotes a box, insert and sleeve. Both call their unit a “set.” If your jewelry collection also needs a care card and a message card, neither price tells you what one finished gift will cost. Define the set first, find the missing work, then compare the revised totals.

Define what the packing team must receive

Give both suppliers the same drawing and artwork revisions, dimensions, material, decoration and quantity per design. A proposed substitute belongs on a separate line: record what changed, then check whether it still holds and presents the same jewelry. A folding carton delivered flat and a mailer with a fitted insert can leave different work for the packing team.

For this fictional example, write the requirement as one sentence: “Each of the 500 sets needs one outer box, one insert, one sleeve, one care card and one message card.” That means 500 pieces of each design, or 2,500 component pieces in total. A quote for “1,000 cards” still needs to say how many of each artwork it covers.

A covers 1,000 component pieces and excludes 1,500. B covers 1,500 and excludes 1,000. Neither includes 500 complete sets. Send each supplier the question its offer leaves open:

  • To A: “Please price 500 sleeves S1, 500 care cards C1 and 500 message cards C2. Does your assembly price cover fitting the insert and positioning both cards?”
  • To B: “Please price 500 each of C1 and C2. Does adding both cards change the assembly charge or the supplied state?”

Excluded describes something the supplier says it will leave out, such as the care card in both offers. Unknown means you still need an answer, such as the charge for adding that card. Record zero only when the supplier confirms there is no additional charge.

Resolve the delivery and payment gaps

Ask each supplier to describe the handover: “We receive the formed box with the insert fitted and both cards loose.” Request carton count, packed dimensions and weight for exactly that state. If the other supplier delivers flat components, freight and packing work may both change.

Record the proposed delivery rule, its version and named place. Incoterms allocate delivery tasks, costs and risks. The goods, their quantities, prices and payment terms still need to appear in the quotation. International Trade Administration guide to Incoterms.

Keep the original currency, quote date, validity and sample timing with each offer. For a planning conversion, record the rate direction, source and date in a separate field. Questions about comparing packaging quotes

Should I compare unit prices first?

Confirm the unit and included components first. A per-box figure cannot be ranked against a per-set figure until their scopes align.

What if two suppliers propose different materials?

Keep the proposals separate and ask how each meets the same requirement. A similar name or matching thickness alone does not establish equivalence.

Can I compare different quantities?

Show them as different scenarios. Request the same quantity and design split where possible, while preserving the original offers.

Does an Incoterm include every project cost?

It allocates defined responsibilities under its scope. Components, payment, separate charges and unresolved exclusions still need their own written agreement.
